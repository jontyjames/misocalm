## 1. Reddit DM to Someone Who Posted About Struggling

### When to use
- Someone posts about a hard moment with misophonia (mealtime blowup, relationship strain, feeling alone)
- The post feels raw and personal, not just a general question
- Use in **Week 1-2** when you are building presence and just showing up as a person
- Do NOT include any links in this message

### The template

> Hey, I saw your post about [specific thing they mentioned, e.g. "dreading family dinners" or "feeling guilty about snapping at your partner"]. I deal with the same thing and have for years. Just wanted you to know you're not alone in this. It's one of the most isolating things, but there are a lot of us out here who get it. Wishing you a gentler week.

### What to customize
- Reference the **specific detail** from their post. "Your post about struggling" is generic and feels like a form letter. "Your post about wearing headphones at every meal and your mum thinking you're rude" shows you actually read it.
- Match their energy. If they were angry, don't be overly soft. If they were sad, don't be overly cheerful.
- Keep your own story brief. This message is about them, not you.

### Common responses and how to handle them

**"Thanks, that means a lot"**
> "Of course. If you ever want to talk about it with someone who gets it, I'm around."

(Let them come to you. Don't push further.)

**"Do you have any advice?"**
> "Honestly, the biggest thing that helped me was starting to notice my patterns. Not to fix them, just to understand them. Like I realized my triggers are way worse when I'm already stressed or tired. That awareness alone changed how I related to it. I also built some daily habits around breathing and journaling that help me reset. Happy to share more if you want."

(If they ask what habits, THEN you can mention MisoCalm naturally: "I actually built a free app for this, funny enough. It started as a personal project because nothing out there felt like it was made by someone who actually has misophonia.")

**No response**
- Normal. Don't follow up. You planted a seed. Move on.

## 2. Reddit Comment on a "Looking for Help" Post

### When to use
- Someone posts asking for coping strategies, tools, or advice
- Posts titled things like "Does anything actually help?" or "How do you deal with this?" or "New here, looking for advice"
- Use from **Week 2 onward** after you have some comment history in the sub

### The template

> For me, the biggest shift was when I stopped trying to fight the triggers and started getting curious about them instead. Not in a clinical way, just noticing. Like I started tracking when my worst moments happened and realized almost all of them were when I was already stressed, under-slept, or in situations where I felt trapped.
>
> That awareness didn't make the triggers go away. But it gave me a tiny gap between the sound and the rage, and that gap is where everything changed.
>
> Practically: physiological sighing (double inhale through the nose, long exhale through the mouth) is the fastest thing I've found for calming the nervous system in the moment. And keeping a simple trigger journal helped me spot patterns I never would have seen otherwise.
>
> I actually built a free app for this called MisoCalm, because nothing out there felt like it was made by someone who actually has misophonia. It has the breathing practices, trigger tracking, and journaling all in one place. Happy to share the link if you want it.

### What to customize
- Lead with **your own specific experience**, not generic advice. The more specific you are about your own patterns, the more it resonates.
- Swap in the coping strategy that's most relevant to what they asked about. If they're asking about relationships, talk about communication. If they're asking about work, talk about environmental strategies.
- Only mention MisoCalm if it's relevant to what they're asking. If they're asking about therapy recommendations, don't shoehorn the app in.

### Common responses and how to handle them

**"Can you share the link?"**
> "Here you go: [link]. It's completely free, no ads, no paywalls on the core tools. I'd love to hear what you think of it. Built it because I needed it, and now I'm trying to get it into the hands of people who might benefit."

**"I've tried everything and nothing works"**
> "I hear you. And I'm not going to pretend there's a magic fix because there isn't. For me it's more like... the intensity of the reaction is still there, but I've learned to relate to it differently. Less 'why is this happening to me' and more 'okay, my nervous system is doing its thing, what do I need right now.' It's not perfect. But it's a lot less lonely when you have some tools and some people who get it."

**"Is this just another meditation app?"**
> "Fair question. It's not a meditation app. It's built specifically for misophonia by someone who has it. The breathing practices are the ones that actually work for fight-or-flight responses, not general mindfulness. The trigger tracking helps you see patterns over time. The journaling prompts are about processing what living with this is actually like. And it doesn't do the whole 'streak' thing or guilt you into using it. It just... meets you where you are."

## 3. Facebook Group Introduction Post

### When to use
- After joining a new misophonia Facebook group
- Wait at least **one full week** of observing and commenting before posting
- Check the group rules first. Some groups have specific introduction threads.
- If there's an intro thread, use a shorter version there instead of a standalone post

### The template

> Hi everyone. I've been lurking here for a bit and figured it's time to say hello.
>
> I'm Jonty. I've had misophonia for about [X] years. It started with eating sounds at the dinner table when I was a kid, and for a long time I thought I was just an angry person. Finding out there was a word for it was one of the most validating moments of my life.
>
> Over the years I've tried a lot of things. Some helped, some didn't. The things that made the biggest difference for me: learning to notice my patterns instead of just reacting, daily breathing practices (especially the physiological sigh), and honestly just connecting with other people who understand. That last one is hard to overstate.
>
> I spend a lot of my time working on tools and resources for people with misophonia. It's become something I care about deeply, not just for myself but for everyone navigating this.
>
> Glad to be here. Looking forward to learning from all of you.

### What to customize
- Your specific trigger origin story. Be real. People connect with specifics.
- What has actually helped you. Not a laundry list. Two or three things that feel true.
- Don't mention MisoCalm in your introduction post. That comes later, after people know you. Your introduction is about being a person, not promoting anything.

### Common responses and how to handle them

**"Welcome! What tools do you work on?"**
> "Thanks! I built a free app called MisoCalm. It has breathing practices, trigger tracking, and journaling, all designed specifically for misophonia. I started building it because nothing out there felt like it was made by someone who actually lives with this. Happy to share more about it if anyone's curious."

**"I relate to so much of this"**
> "That's what I've found again and again: our stories are different but the core experience is so similar. The anger, the guilt, the isolation. You're in good company here."

**No engagement**
- Don't take it personally. Post timing matters a lot on Facebook. Continue engaging with other people's posts and your presence will grow naturally.

## 4. DM to Someone Interested in the App

### When to use
- Someone comments "I'd love to try this" or "Can you send me the link?" or asks a follow-up question about MisoCalm
- Someone DMs you asking about it
- Be prompt. Interest is perishable. Respond within a few hours if possible.

### The template

> Hey! Glad you're interested. Here's the link to MisoCalm: [link]
>
> Quick tips for getting started: the onboarding takes about 2 minutes and it asks you about your triggers so it can personalize things a bit. After that, I'd suggest trying one of the breathing practices first. The physiological sigh one is my go-to when I'm actually triggered. The trigger journal is great for building awareness over time.
>
> There's no wrong way to use it. Some people use it daily, some just pull it up in tough moments. It's there when you need it.
>
> I'd genuinely love to hear how it feels. I built this because I needed it myself, and every bit of feedback helps me make it better for our community.

### What to customize
- If they mentioned a specific struggle, point them to the relevant feature. "Since you mentioned mealtime being hard, the trigger journal might be a good starting point. Even just logging a few moments can help you see what's going on beneath the surface."
- If they're a parent, adjust the tone: "Your child can go through the onboarding themselves, and the breathing practices are gentle enough for younger users."

### Common responses and how to handle them

**"This is really nice" / "I love the design"**
> "That means a lot, thank you. I put a lot of care into making it feel like a calm space, not another app demanding things from you. Would love to hear how it goes after you've used it a few times."

**"I tried it but I'm not sure how to use the trigger journal"**
> "Totally fair. The trigger journal is simple by design. You just log what happened: what the sound was, where you were, how intense it felt, and what was going on around you. Over time, patterns start to show up that you'd never notice otherwise. Things like 'my reactions are always worse at night' or 'it's not all sounds, it's mostly this one person in this one context.' That awareness is the foundation for everything else."

**Radio silence after sending the link**
- Send one gentle follow-up after about a week: "Hey, just checking in. No pressure at all. If you got a chance to try MisoCalm I'd love to hear your thoughts. And if the timing wasn't right, totally understand. It's there whenever you need it."
- After that, let it be.

## 5. Asking for a Testimonial

### When to use
- Someone has been using MisoCalm for at least a week or two
- They've said something positive about it (even casually)
- They seem engaged and willing
- **Don't ask someone who's in a hard moment.** Wait for a neutral or positive interaction.

### The template

> Hey [name], I really appreciate you using MisoCalm and the kind things you've said about it. I'm collecting feedback from people to help others understand what the app is like, and I was wondering if you'd be up for sharing a few words?
>
> No pressure at all. If you're open to it, here are a few questions you could pick from (you don't have to answer all of them, even one or two sentences would mean the world):
>
> - What was going on with your misophonia before you started using the app?
> - What part of MisoCalm has been most helpful for you?
> - Has anything shifted in how you relate to your triggers?
> - Would you recommend it to someone else with misophonia?
>
> You can keep it as short or long as you want. And if you'd prefer to stay anonymous, that's completely fine. I'll never share anything without your permission.
>
> Either way, thank you for being part of this. It matters more than you know.

### What to customize
- Reference something specific they said or did. "When you mentioned the breathing practice helping during your commute, that stuck with me."
- If they're very active, acknowledge that: "You've been using it consistently and I can tell you're really engaging with it."

### Common responses and how to handle them

**"Sure! What do you want me to say?"**
> "Honestly, just whatever feels true to you. The questions I sent are starting points, but your own words in your own voice are what matters. A few sentences is more than enough."

**"I'm not great at writing things"**
> "No worries at all. Would it be easier if I asked you a couple of questions and you just replied naturally? Or if you'd rather, you could send a quick voice note and I'll transcribe it. Whatever feels comfortable."

**"I like it but I don't think I've used it enough to say much"**
> "That's completely fair. No rush at all. If at some point down the road you feel like sharing, I'd love to hear from you. And honestly, even 'I've been using it for a few days and the breathing practices help' is valuable. But genuinely no pressure."

>

### What to customize
- Reference the specific thing they've said that suggests they're ready. "When you mentioned wanting to understand why certain people trigger you more than others, that's exactly the kind of thing we dig into in the course."

### Common responses and how to handle them

The community gives you the things tools can't: a structured path, live human connection with people who understand, and the kind of accountability that comes from doing this alongside others. I used tools on their own for years and they helped in the moment. What actually shifted things for me was not doing it alone anymore."

**"I'm in!"**
> "That means a lot. Genuinely. Welcome. When you get set up, take your time with the first module. And the next live call is [date/time]. Looking forward to having you there."

**"Not right now, but maybe later"**

## 7. Email to a Therapist or Audiologist

### When to use
- Reaching out to therapists who list misophonia or sensory processing as a specialty
- Audiologists who work with sound sensitivity
- Therapists mentioned in misophonia communities or directories
- **Do your research first.** Mention something specific about their practice if possible.

### The template

> Subject: Free resource for your clients with misophonia
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm Jonty. I've been living with misophonia for [X] years, and over the past couple of years I've been building tools to help people in our community.
>
> The main one is MisoCalm, a free app designed specifically for people with misophonia. It includes breathing practices calibrated for fight-or-flight regulation, a trigger journal for pattern awareness, guided journaling, and calming experiences. It's completely free with no ads, no data selling, and no paywalls on the core tools.
>
> I thought it might be useful as a between-sessions resource for your clients who deal with sound sensitivity. A lot of people tell me the hardest part of misophonia is the time between appointments when they're on their own with their triggers. MisoCalm is designed to be a daily companion for those moments.
>
> I'd be happy to walk you through the app or send you a brief overview you could share with clients. Whatever would be most helpful.
>
> Thank you for the work you do. Therapists who understand misophonia are rare, and your clients are lucky to have you.
>
> Warm regards,
> Jonty
> [link to MisoCalm]

### What to customize
- **Research them.** If they've published articles, mention one. If they have a specific approach (CBT, ACT, somatic), reference how MisoCalm complements it.
- For audiologists, adjust the language: "I know many of your patients with sound sensitivity may be looking for complementary tools alongside clinical treatment."
- If they're connected to a misophonia organization, mention it: "I saw your work with [organization] and wanted to reach out."

### Common responses and how to handle them

**"This looks great, can you tell me more?"**
> "Of course. MisoCalm works like this: [brief walkthrough of features]. The philosophy behind it is empathy-first. No gamification, no streak pressure, no clinical coldness. It's designed to feel like a calm, understanding companion. I'd be happy to jump on a quick call if that would be easier than email."

**"Can I share this with my clients?"**
> "Absolutely. That's exactly what I'm hoping for. Here's the link: [link]. If it would be helpful, I can put together a one-page overview you could print out or share digitally. And I'm always open to feedback from clinicians. If your clients have suggestions for what would make the app more useful alongside therapy, I'd love to hear them."

**No response**
- Follow up once after 7-10 days: "Hi [Name], just circling back on my note about MisoCalm. I know your inbox is probably full. If this isn't relevant for your practice, no worries at all. But if it could help your clients, I'd love to chat. Either way, thank you for the work you do."
- After two emails, let it go. Not everyone will respond and that's okay.

## General Principles (Read These First)

### The Golden Rules of Outreach

1. **Give before you ask.** Every interaction should offer something: empathy, a tip, your presence, your story. The app mention is never the point. It's a natural extension of the help you're already giving.

2. **Be specific.** "I know how you feel" is empty. "The thing you said about dreading Sunday dinners because that's when the whole family eats together, I felt that in my body" is real.

3. **Read the room.** If someone is venting, validate. Don't problem-solve. If someone is asking for help, help. Don't just validate. Match what they need, not what you want to say.

4. **One mention per thread.** If you've already mentioned MisoCalm in a thread, don't mention it again. Let it breathe.

5. **Follow up with care.** One follow-up is kind. Two is acceptable. Three is too many. Respect silence.

6. **Never punch down on other tools.** If someone uses a different app or technique, respect that. "I'm glad that's helping you" is always the right response.

7. **Remember the long game.** You're not trying to convert people today. You're building a reputation as someone who genuinely cares about this community. That reputation is worth more than any individual signup.

### Tone Reminders

- Write like a person, not a brand
- Shorter sentences are warmer
- Questions are invitations, not demands
- "No pressure" is only believable when there's genuinely no pressure
- If you wouldn't say it to a friend sitting across from you, don't type it

Keep a simple log. Nothing elaborate. Just enough to know where you are:
